Rialto

Pay a foreign invoice
in one transaction.

A supplier bills you €5,000. You hold dollars. Today that costs 40–60bp through a payments provider or 200–300bp through a bank, takes one to three days, and arrives with nothing linking it back to the invoice.

Rialto settles it onchain in one transaction, at 25bp, at the FX rate that actually exists — and the payee receives exactly €5,000, not "about" €5,000.

Who it is forImporters, exporters and agencies paying cross-border invoices — the businesses too small for a bank's FX desk.
What they getExact settlement in the currency they were billed in, sub-second, with the invoice reference and the rate they were charged recorded onchain.
Where it runsArc, Circle's stablecoin chain, where USDC is the gas token — so the price, the fee and the settlement are one asset.

How one payment works

1The invoice arrives €5,000, ref INV-2026-114, due in 30 days.
2You send one transaction Bounded at the most you will part with, expiring when you say.
3The rate is read on-chain Verified from a publisher quorum, refused if staler than your bound.
4The payee is paid exactly €5,000 lands, and the reference and effective rate are on-chain for reconciliation.

Every currency pair works this way, including ones with no direct pool: routing through USDC means N corridors give N(N−1)/2 pairs, so a Brazilian importer pays a Philippine peso invoice without a BRL/PHP pool existing.

Which part of the payment this is

Rialto is not a payments company and does not pretend to be one. A cross-border payment has three legs — get in, convert, get out — and only the middle one is broken on Arc, because the chain cannot read an FX rate. That leg is the whole product.

Getting in — not usCircle Mint, an exchange, or a USDC balance the business already holds. Circle has spent years on this and it works.
The FX leg — thisUSDC to the exact euro amount billed, at the real rate, 25bp, one transaction, invoice reference on-chain. Nothing on Arc could do this, because nothing on Arc could read a price.
Getting out — not usThe payee holds EURC, or cashes to a local account through a partner. Several of them are already Circle grantees.

So the honest scope: no KYC, no fiat ramps, no local payout, no licence to hold client money. Those are someone else's competence and Circle already funds that network. What that network cannot do today is convert at a rate the chain can verify, and that is the gap every corridor below runs through.

The other half: locking the rate

An invoice has terms. "€5,000, due in 30 days" means the payer finds out what they owe a month after agreeing the price, and over a month EUR/USD moves a percent or two — the emerging-market corridors below move 14 to 18 percent a year. Against that, being 25bp cheaper than a bank is noise. A business does not lie awake over a quarter of a percent; it lies awake over the three percent it cannot predict. So the second half of the product is a rate lock, collateralised and cash-settled in USDC.

What feeds it, and what that is not. One command fetches the current ECB reference rate and posts it — that is the whole publisher, and the cost above is the real transaction. But the ECB publishes once a day while the pool settles against a 15-minute bound, so the publisher re-attests the same value every ten minutes to keep the feed inside it: about 0.13 USDC a day, and strictly a daily number held inside an intraday bound. A production feed takes intraday quotes from several independent publishers — which is also the only thing that makes an M-of-N quorum mean anything, since a quorum of one publisher reading one source is a trusted feed with extra steps. Both limitations are in REVIEW.md; neither is solved by this deployment.

How it makes money

Half the 25bp goes to the liquidity providers who carry the inventory; half goes to the protocol. That split is enforced on-chain — protocolSharePpm is capped at half by the contract and the protocol's share can only ever be collected to the treasury address. 12.5bp of everything settled.

Why nobody had built this

Circle has announced partner stablecoins in BRL, MXN, PHP, ZAR, JPY, KRW, CAD and AUD. Those trade at roughly 5, 17, 57, 16, 150, 1400, 1.4 and 1.5 to the dollar. The only venue design deployed on Arc is a Curve-style stableswap, whose invariant is flat — cheap — around equal balances, meaning a price of 1.00. Nothing there is near 1.00, and no pool can be centred on a rate the chain cannot read.

What is built but not yet exercised on-chain

Everything above ran on Arc testnet today. One piece has not, and saying which is more useful than implying all of it has.

RialtoRouter — multi-hop exact-output settlement, so N corridors give N(N−1)/2 pairs instead of N² pools: a Brazilian importer settles a peso invoice with no BRL/PHP pool in existence. It is deployed and source-verified, and covered by ten tests including the ones that reject a repeated pool and a disjoint path. What it has not done is route a live trade, because that needs a second seeded pool and testnet EURC is metered at 20 per request. It is the first thing a grant would buy.

71 tests · 3 reproducible measurements · an adversarial review that found and fixed an unbounded oracle failure mode, and states what remains exploitable.

What a grant buys, in order

Everything above runs on a book seeded from a single faucet drip. The design is not the constraint; depth and independence are.

  • A second corridor, so the router settles a live multi-hop trade — the one path here that has not run on-chain.
  • Independent publishers. A quorum of one reading one source is a trusted feed with extra steps, and it stays one until somebody else is signing.
  • An intraday source, so the feed stops being a daily reference rate held inside a fifteen-minute bound.
  • Inventory, because 2bp of slippage on a EUR 20 book is not the number that matters — the number that matters is what a EUR 500,000 invoice costs.
