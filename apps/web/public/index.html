Rialto · stablecoin FX on Arc

Arc ships with no price oracle.
So every FX pool on it is centred at 1.00.

Circle has announced partner stablecoins in BRL, MXN, PHP, ZAR, JPY, KRW, CAD and AUD. Those trade at roughly 5, 17, 57, 16, 150, 1400, 1.4 and 1.5 to the dollar — nowhere near 1.00. We measured what that costs, over a year of real ECB observations, on a real EVM.

A year of LP returns, by corridor

Two pools identical in every respect except where their liquidity sits — same invariant, amplification, fee and trades. 259 ECB observations, sampled weekly.

Sampled weekly rather than daily, which gives both pools a fifth of the retail flow and so a fifth of the fee income. That makes the rate-anchored column look worse than the daily EUR/USD run, where it returns +2.32%. The comparison between the two is unaffected — both see identical trades.

A third of the liquidity provider's capital, per year. On EUR/USD the incumbent design is merely wrong; on the corridors stablecoin payments are actually for, it is a shredder.

This is not a criticism of anyone's pool

ViFi Labs and Lunex are both live on Arc testnet building FX, and both work under the same constraint we did: there is no price feed on Arc. Circle's own prediction-market sample has to bootstrap UMA for the same reason. You cannot centre a pool on a rate the chain cannot read, so every design has to approximate one — and the table above is what the approximation costs, whoever deployed the pool.

So we built the oracle first, and it is permissionless to read. It makes our pools work. It would make theirs work too.

What is deployed

RialtoOracle — pull-based signed FX feed, M-of-N publishers, deviation ceiling. RialtoPool — stableswap on rate-scaled balances. RialtoSettlement — exact-output invoice settlement with the reference on-chain. RialtoRouter — multi-hop, so N corridors give N(N−1)/2 pairs. RialtoForward — collateralised rate locks, because an invoice has terms.
